## Key guardrails

- Destination blindness is the central product requirement during comparisons, with one accepted tradeoff: destination photography may be used as a soft visual cue. Names, countries, flags, airport codes, maps, scores, ranks, and photo-credit metadata remain hidden while playing. Once a participant completes the game, they may browse an unranked named destination atlas with general context, real map placement, and credited photography, and may privately see only their own top five. Every other traveler’s shortlist and all group outcomes remain embargoed until Dan opens the result reveal.
- The ranking engine belongs in `backend/`; shared contracts belong in `shared/`.
- Group results are revealed only after all five fixed roster members finish and Dan opens the gate. Each person's inferred ranks one through five contribute `5, 4, 3, 2, 1` points, respectively; lower ranks contribute zero. Ties resolve by first-place votes, then top-five appearances, then remain shared. The social reveal shows all five personal top fives and evidence-backed overlap/divergence notes, never activity-by-activity choices or normalized group utilities.
- `seed/activity-media.json` is the private credit catalog for opaque comparison-card paths. Comparison responses contain no destination, coordinate, score, rank, or credit field; destination names, maps, galleries, and credits are exposed only in the completion-gated atlas and post-gate reveal.
- `assets/` contains supplied traveler artwork approved only for roster, progress, waiting, and celebratory UI. Never use it in destination-blind activity cards; the logo and all visual tokens live in `design-system/`.

## 2026-08-23 Lightning Round follow-up

- The original five-person round has now completed and its first envelope has been opened. Never reset, rewrite, or use the original `lgsV4Users`, `lgsV4State/reveal`, or `lgsV4ResultSnapshots` data for testing.
- The direct-destination Lightning Round is an intentional post-reveal sibling experience. It is explicitly allowed to show place names, countries, photography, activities, weather, travel effort, planning fare estimates, and caveats because the original anonymity gate is already over.
- `seed/lightning-round/destination-briefs.json` is its content source. `bayes-direct-destination-v1` asks 48 fair-coverage direct comparisons, then at most 12 posterior-selected tie-breakers; it returns visible shared tiers when adjacent places fail the 85% separation rule. The group result is a transparent 24-to-1 Borda tally, with shared tiers splitting positions evenly.
- After viewing that private direct list, every Lightning traveler explicitly submits zero to four immutable personal vetoes: named places they would not take. A veto is an advisory conversation signal only. It never changes the direct model, a personal rank, shared tiers, Borda points, or group order. A traveler is ready for the second envelope only after submitting the veto step, including an explicit empty submission.
- A completed Lightning traveler can see a caller-only ordered direct decision trail (`winnerId`, `loserId`, and order) alongside their private full list. It is derived from their saved comparisons, is not copied into the immutable group snapshot, and is presented as a factual “X beat Y” record, never as an elimination bracket. After the second envelope opens, the group snapshot includes each member’s submitted veto IDs and a derived per-place veto summary; the sortable five-person table shows `Vetoed` in place of that member’s rank. These signals remain sealed until the second envelope.
- Lightning data must stay isolated in `lgsV4LightningUsers`, `lgsV4LightningState/round`, and `lgsV4LightningResultSnapshots`. Its initialization requires an original immutable reveal snapshot. Do not add Lightning state to original reset/preflight tooling. Never retrofit vetoes into an already-opened Lightning snapshot; a legacy snapshot simply has no collected vetoes.
- Lightning navigation is a separate phase-aware context, not an addition to the original result menu. Once the original envelope is open, the explicit `Round 1` / `Lightning Round` switcher is the only cross-round bridge. Lightning introduction, direct choices, and unsaved-veto states use only a compact focus header with truthful status and a `Round 1 results` exit. After a veto save, Lightning gets its own menu: **My full list**, **Who’s ready**, **About this round**, and, only after the second envelope opens, **How everyone ranked**. Keep its hashes (`#lightning`, `#lightning-list`, `#lightning-veto`, `#lightning-ready`, `#lightning-reveal`, and `#lightning-help`) separate from original hashes and resolve them through server status.
